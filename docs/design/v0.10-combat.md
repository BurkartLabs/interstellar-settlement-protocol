# The combat model: units, counters, accuracy and evasion, upkeep (ISP-164, AB#858)

v0.10 Warfare (Feature AB#394) needs a combat model before any of its code: DEVELOPMENT_PHASES.md lists
rock-paper-scissors counters and accuracy and evasion stats, and the design goal is that combat is *"almost entirely
deterministic/numbers-based, with slight influence based on bonuses for accuracy and dodging"*
(.github/custom-instructions.md, "Direct Combat"). The resolver this replaces summed attack and defense and multiplied
them by a modifier the caller supplied (`POST /api/combat/simulate`, once `server/index.js:3359`, removed for Bug
#1036). Without a counter system, one composition dominates. Here, for any composition there is another that beats it.

It changes no code. The numbers live in one runnable file,
[`scripts/combat-model.mjs`](../../scripts/combat-model.mjs) (`npm run combat:model` prints every table below, beside
`economy:model` and `belt:model`). The resolver story (ISP-168, AB#862) reproduces those tables in its tests. It was read against ISP main
`e7da8361`, 2026-09-27.

**Never for real money.** The Board ruled on AB#706: no v0.10 military mechanic may ever be sold or accelerated for real
money. That covers units, troops, build and training time, upkeep and readiness, and everything else in this file
(BRIEF.md, rule 3). No number here has a paid path.

**Chosen, not derived.** Every stat and cost below was tuned by the calculator against the rules in section 5, and the
Board has not seen them. ECONOMY.md will list them once the catalog lands as data (ISP-167, AB#861).

## 1. The units

A unit's **defense** is its hit points: the damage it absorbs before it is lost. **Attack** is the damage it deals per
round before counters and accuracy. **Supply** is what it counts towards the fleet size that upkeep is charged on
(section 7). Value is the credits plus the goods at their NPC standing bid (data/economy.json), and is what the
budgets in section 5 spend.

| id | Unit | Credits | Goods | Value | Build time | Built at | Research | Supply | Upkeep a day | Attack | Defense (hp) | Accuracy | Evasion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lf` | Light fighter | 1,150 | 10 Carbon Fibre Sheets | 1,360 | 30 min | Orbital Shipyard | `tt-fleet-coordination` | 1 | 20 | 12 | 40 | 11 | 10 |
| `ds` | Destroyer | 3,350 | 8 Armour Plating | 4,294 | 2 h 30 | Orbital Shipyard | `tt-capital-hulls` (new) | 4 | 80 | 34 | 200 | 10 | 5 |
| `se` | Siege engine | 3,600 | 8 Pressure Vessels | 4,632 | 3 h | Orbital Shipyard | `tt-siege-ordnance` (new) | 5 | 100 | 60 | 170 | 8 | 3 |
| `at` | Attack troops (a squad) | 700 | 2 Battery Cells | 946 | 20 min | Headquarters | `tt-fleet-coordination` | 1 | 12 | 10 | 30 | 9 | 7 |
| `dt` | Defense troops (a squad) | 800 | 6 Structural Alloys | 950 | 20 min | Headquarters | none | 1 | 8 | 8 | 40 | 9 | 6 |

What each one is for:
- **Light fighter:** fast, evasive and cheap. It kills siege engines, which cannot track it, and dies to destroyers'
  flak.
- **Destroyer:** the line ship, armoured, with flak that shreds fighters. Boarding parties take it.
- **Siege engine:** the heaviest gun and the slowest hull. It breaks entrenched defense troops, and fighters get under
  its guns.
- **Attack troops:** marines in assault transports. They board and take destroyers, and are repelled by defense troops.
- **Defense troops:** the garrison. They hold against boarders, and siege guns dig them out.

**Where they are built.**
- Ships come from the **Orbital Shipyard**, a corporation building that ISP-170 (AB#864) makes buildable, id
  `orbital-shipyard`.
- Troops train at the corporation's **Headquarters**. Every corporation already has one (server/game/corporation.js:30)
  and nothing reads it yet (docs/design/buildings.md). docs/design/buildings.md reads DEVELOPMENT_PHASES.md's "attack
  and defense troop building" as *building troops*, not a building type, so troops get no new building.
- One production queue per building, one unit at a time. Build times are fixed and shown before the order (ISP-171,
  AB#865; ISP-172, AB#866).

**Research gates.**
- `tt-fleet-coordination` exists (tier 2, Military, 12,000 credits, 16 h). It gates light fighters and attack troops,
  so a corporation's first military costs a tier-2 project, not the whole tree.
- The new ids are added with the catalog (ISP-167, AB#861) and belong to Military:
  - `tt-capital-hulls`: tier 3, 16,000 credits, 18 h; prereqs `tt-fleet-coordination` and `tt-material-compression`.
    Armour Plating comes from the titanium chain, which needs the latter.
  - `tt-siege-ordnance`: tier 3, 20,000 credits, 20 h; prereqs `tt-capital-hulls` and `tt-ferrous-metallurgy`.
    Pressure Vessels need the latter.
- **Defense troops need no research.** A corporation that has never touched the Military tree can still garrison its
  own station. Defense is never locked behind a project.
- Accuracy and evasion research (`tt-fire-control`, `tt-evasive-manoeuvres`) is ISP-169's (AB#863), with the limits in
  section 3.

**The fleet cap.** Ships share the Fleet Cap with mining probes: it is one fleet, and ISP-171 (AB#865) counts every
ship the corporation has and every ship its shipyard is building against it (server/game/progression.js fleetStatus).
Troops are not vessels and do not count. The cap is a ceiling on hulls. What decides a fleet's size below it is upkeep
(section 7), which is a curve, not a wall, so it is a decision (T-018). *(Changed by ISP-171: an earlier version of
this section kept combat units off the Fleet Cap; the story's acceptance caps production by it.)*

**Production** (ISP-171). Ships are queued at the Orbital Shipyard, one job per ship type and quantity (at most 100),
at most 10 jobs deep. A job is paid in full when queued, in credits and in goods from the station the corporation is
docked at. Jobs run one after another, each taking quantity x build time, and deliver on the server's fabrication
tick. **Cancelling** returns all of a job not yet started, and half of one already under way (credits and goods,
rounded down), since the yard has worked the materials. The jobs behind it move up.

## 2. The counter matrix

Each unit is strong against exactly one other and weak against exactly one, in a five-way cycle:

**light fighter > siege engine > defense troops > attack troops > destroyer > light fighter**

The multiplier a shooter's damage takes against a target (row shoots column):

| shooter \ target | lf | ds | se | at | dt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **lf** | 1 | 0.5 | **2** | 1 | 1 |
| **ds** | **2** | 1 | 1 | 0.5 | 1 |
| **se** | 0.5 | 1 | 1 | 1 | **2** |
| **at** | 1 | **2** | 1 | 1 | 0.5 |
| **dt** | 1 | 1 | 0.5 | **2** | 1 |

Strong is 2, weak is 0.5 and everything else is 1. The pair is symmetric: if A deals double to B, B deals half to A.

**Why every unit can go anywhere.** "Attack" and "defense" troops name their combat role, as Travian's troops do, not
where they may fight. Defense troops can escort a fleet (against boarders), and attack troops can stand in a garrison.
This is forced, not chosen. Suppose attack troops could only attack and defense troops could only defend:

1. Defense troops must prey on something. If that prey can defend, a garrison made of it has no attacker able to
   counter it. So defense troops must prey on the one unit that never defends, attack troops.
2. By the same argument from the other side, attack troops must prey on defense troops.
3. That makes a two-unit loop inside the five-unit cycle, which is impossible.

The calculator showed the failure directly. With attack troops allowed to defend but defense troops fixed at home, an
all-attack-troops garrison could only ever be fought to a draw.

## 3. Accuracy and evasion

**hit fraction = accuracy ÷ (accuracy + evasion)**

This uses the shooter's accuracy and the target's evasion, and is applied to the expected damage directly. There is no
roll: a shooter whose accuracy equals its target's evasion lands exactly half its damage, every time. There are no dice
anywhere in combat, so there is no seed to explain.

- The spread is deliberately narrow (accuracy 8 to 11, evasion 3 to 10). Hit fractions run from 0.44 (a siege engine
  on a fighter) to 0.79 (a fighter on a siege engine). That is the Board's *slight* influence, next to the counters' 4×
  swing from weak to strong.
- **Modifiers** multiply a side's accuracy, evasion or attack:
  - research (ISP-169, AB#863): Fire-Control Computers (`tt-fire-control`) +10% accuracy, and Evasive Manoeuvres
    (`tt-evasive-manoeuvres`) +10% evasion. Both are tier 3 Military after `tt-fleet-coordination`, planned until a
    battle can be fought;
  - the CEO's leadership, `corp.military.modifiers.ceoLeadershipPct`, which adds its percentage to both accuracy and
    evasion. Nothing raises it yet, so it is 0 for every corporation until a CEO program does;
  - readiness (section 7), which multiplies attack and accuracy only.

  Research and CEO bonuses together are capped at +30% per stat, so no bonus stack turns a counter matchup around. A
  2× counter is worth more than any legal bonus. `combatModifiersFor(corp)` in server/game/combat.js reads them from the
  corporation's state, and the resolver holds anything passed to it to the same cap. Measured: 10 fighters at the cap
  outlast 11 without it and lose to 15, so the bonuses are worth about a tenth more ships. At the cap, each pure
  composition's counter is still its best answer, with an exchange above 3, even when the prey has every bonus and
  the counter has none.

## 4. Resolution

A battle is between an attacking side and a defending side, each a count per unit type, plus each side's modifiers.

```
for round 1 to 6, while both sides have a unit type with at least 0.5 units:
  for each side S firing at the other side T, from the counts at the start of the round:
    total_T = sum of T's counts (types with at least 0.5 units)
    for each type s in S, for each type t in T:
      damage[t] += count_S[s] × attack[s] × mod_S.attack
                   × (count_T[t] / total_T)                    // fire is shared by head-count, as OGame's random targeting is on average
                   × counter(s, t)                             // section 2
                   × hit(accuracy[s] × mod_S.accuracy, evasion[t] × mod_T.evasion)   // section 3
  for each type t on each side:
    lost[t] = min(count[t], damage[t] ÷ hp[t])                 // fractional: expected losses, not dice
    count[t] -= lost[t]; a count below 0.5 becomes 0
winner: the side with units left when the other has none. Otherwise, after round 6, a draw, and the attacker withdraws.
survivors: each type rounded to the nearest whole unit (a half rounds up, in the survivor's favour).
```

The rules that matter:
- **Fire is simultaneous.** Both sides shoot from the start-of-round counts, so the order of the sides changes nothing.
- **Counts are fractional inside the battle.** A unit's expected losses are taken exactly, so the same battle gives the
  same result, and a destroyer at 40% health is 0.4 of a destroyer rather than a coin flip. Only the report rounds.
- **Six rounds, then a draw.** This is OGame's limit, and it stops a battle between two walls from running forever. A
  draw is a failed raid: nothing is looted (ISP-165, AB#859, owns what a raid takes).
- **Fodder is real.** Fire is shared by head-count, so cheap numerous units soak fire meant for expensive ones. That is
  a legitimate tactic and a counter-play, not a bug: fighters screening siege engines is a real composition (section 5).

**Lanchester's square law** falls out of this. Damage dealt is proportional to the number still firing, and losses
reduce that number. A force's strength therefore goes as its size squared times the quality of its units. In the
calculator, 20 destroyers against 10 win in five rounds and lose 3 (√(20² − 10²) ≈ 17.3 remain). Doubling a fleet more
than doubles what it can beat, which is why size needs a cost that also grows faster than linearly (section 7).

## 5. No dominant composition

Three budgets (30,000, 120,000 and 480,000 of value a side), each composition bought in whole units. The candidates
are the five pure compositions and the ten even pairs.

## 6. A worked battle

A raid: **6 destroyers and 12 attack troops** attack a station held by **14 light fighters and 10 defense troops**.
Round 1 is worked by hand below. Totals are 18 attacking units and 24 defending, and nobody has modifiers.

**Attacker's fire.**
- Destroyers fire 6 × 34 = 204:
  - At the light fighters, 204 × (14/24) × 2 (strong) × 10/(10+10) = 204 × 0.5833 × 2 × 0.5 = **119.00**.
  - At the defense troops, 204 × (10/24) × 1 × 10/(10+6) = 204 × 0.4167 × 0.625 = **53.13**.
- Attack troops fire 12 × 10 = 120:
  - At the light fighters, 120 × 0.5833 × 1 × 9/(9+10) = **33.16**.
  - At the defense troops, 120 × 0.4167 × 0.5 (weak) × 9/(9+6) = **15.00**.
- Light fighters take 152.16 damage ÷ 40 hp = **3.80 lost**. Defense troops take 68.13 ÷ 40 = **1.70 lost**.

**Defender's fire.**
- Light fighters fire 14 × 12 = 168:
  - At the destroyers, 168 × (6/18) × 0.5 (weak) × 11/(11+5) = **19.25**.
  - At the attack troops, 168 × (12/18) × 1 × 11/(11+7) = **68.44**.
- Defense troops fire 10 × 8 = 80:
  - At the destroyers, 80 × 0.3333 × 1 × 9/(9+5) = **17.14**.
  - At the attack troops, 80 × 0.6667 × 2 (strong) × 9/(9+7) = **60.00**.
- Destroyers take 36.39 ÷ 200 = **0.18 lost**. Attack troops take 128.44 ÷ 30 = **4.28 lost**.

The whole battle, from the calculator:

| Round | Destroyers | Attack troops | Light fighters | Defense troops |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| start | 6.00 | 12.00 | 14.00 | 10.00 |
| 1 | 5.82 (−0.18) | 7.72 (−4.28) | 10.20 (−3.80) | 8.30 (−1.70) |
| 2 | 5.64 (−0.18) | 4.88 (−2.84) | 6.97 (−3.23) | 6.65 (−1.65) |
| 3 | 5.47 (−0.17) | 3.16 (−1.72) | 4.22 (−2.75) | 5.01 (−1.64) |
| 4 | 5.33 (−0.14) | 2.23 (−0.93) | 1.92 (−2.30) | 3.30 (−1.71) |
| 5 | 5.24 (−0.09) | 1.80 (−0.43) | 0 (−1.76, the last 0.16 falls below 0.5) | 1.41 (−1.90) |
| 6 | 5.22 (−0.03) | 1.69 (−0.11) | 0 | 0 (−1.41) |

**The attacker wins in six rounds and keeps 5 destroyers and 2 attack troops.** The destroyers ate the fighters, their
prey. The defense troops ate the attack troops, theirs, but there were too few of them, and every destroyer they
might have boarded stayed afloat. With 15 defense troops in place of the 10, the same raid is a draw: 4 destroyers
limp home, and the station keeps 1 fighter and 4 troops. The defender reinforcing during the warning window (ISP-180,
AB#874) is the counter-play.

## 7. Upkeep, size and readiness (T-018)

Without a rising cost, Lanchester's law makes "as many units as possible" the answer at every scale. That is the
linear-knob trap in T-018: a slider that looks like a decision and contains none. So **daily upkeep grows with the
square of fleet size relative to the corporation's logistics capacity**:

**upkeep a day = Σ (units × base upkeep) × (1 + (S ÷ K)²)**

- S is the corporation's total supply, across all fleets and garrisons.
- K = 40 × the Orbital Shipyard's level (1 to 5).

Total upkeep therefore grows with the cube of fleet size. A bigger shipyard carries a bigger fleet at the same
marginal cost, which is what the shipyard's levels buy.

**The curve.** Assume a fleet earns about r = 45 credits per supply per day. That is an assumption: raids, mercenary
hire and Rat bounties set the real r, and ISP-165 (AB#859) owns them. Base upkeep is u = 20 per supply per day (fighters
20 per supply, destroyers 80 for 4, siege engines 100 for 5; troops are cheaper, 12 and 8). Net is r·S − u·S·(1 + (S/K)²):

| Shipyard level 1 (K 40): S | 10 | 20 | **26** | 30 | 40 | 60 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| earns a day | 450 | 900 | 1,170 | 1,350 | 1,800 | 2,700 | 3,600 |
| upkeep a day | 213 | 500 | 740 | 938 | 1,600 | 3,900 | 8,000 |
| **net** | 238 | 400 | **430** | 413 | 200 | −1,200 | −4,400 |

| Shipyard level 3 (K 120): S | 30 | 60 | **78** | 90 | 120 | 180 | 240 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| earns a day | 1,350 | 2,700 | 3,510 | 4,050 | 5,400 | 8,100 | 10,800 |
| upkeep a day | 638 | 1,500 | 2,219 | 2,813 | 4,800 | 11,700 | 24,000 |
| **net** | 713 | 1,200 | **1,291** | 1,238 | 600 | −3,600 | −13,200 |

**The optimum** is where marginal upkeep meets marginal earning: S* = K·√((r/u − 1)/3). That is 26 supply at level 1
and 78 at level 3. Past twice the optimum the fleet costs more than it earns.
- **Against the economy that pays it:** a corporation's best day today is about 3,500 credits (ECONOMY.md). Level 1's
  optimal fleet costs 740 a day, a fifth of that, which is a real choice and not a rounding error.
- **The shape holds for any r above u.** When AB#859 fixes r, the optimum moves and still exists. If r falls to u or
  below, the right fleet is garrison-only, which is also a legitimate answer.

**Unpaid upkeep degrades readiness. It never deletes a unit and never locks the player out** (ISP-173, AB#867):
- Upkeep is charged daily from credits, with the payroll (its own ledger category, `military-upkeep`).
- If credits cannot cover it, the corporation's **readiness** falls 10 points (from 100, to a floor of 40) for each day
  unpaid, and rises 20 points a day once it is paid again.
- Readiness multiplies every one of its units' attack and accuracy (a 60 readiness is × 0.6). It never touches hit
  points or evasion, so an unpaid garrison still stands and still absorbs a raid.
- In the calculator, 10 destroyers at full readiness against 10 at 60% end in a draw after six rounds, 8 against 4.
- Nothing is destroyed or disbanded by debt. The player can always dock, defend, pay, and choose to disband units
  (refunding nothing).
- There is no paid way to restore readiness (AB#706).

## 8. Prior art

- **Lanchester's laws** (F. W. Lanchester, *Aircraft in Warfare*, 1916). With aimed fire, a force's strength is its
  size squared times its unit quality (the square law), because every surviving unit keeps firing. This is why a
  numbers-based resolver makes concentration decisive (section 4), and why size needs a superlinear cost (section 7).
- **OGame's combat.** Up to six rounds, then a draw. Simultaneous fire each round. Targets are chosen in proportion to
  the enemy's unit counts. "Rapid fire": a ship type that gets extra shots against specific targets is how OGame makes
  counters. The model here keeps OGame's six rounds and head-count targeting. It replaces the dice with expected values
  (the Board's "deterministic"), and replaces rapid fire with the 2× and 0.5× counter multiplier.
- **Travian's troop split.** Every unit has one attack value and two defense values, against infantry and against
  cavalry, so a defender picks infantry-defenders or cavalry-defenders against what is coming. That is a three-way
  counter carried by defense types. This model has one defense value but the same lesson: the defender's composition
  should depend on what it expects. Travian's troops, like these, are named for their role, not confined to it.
