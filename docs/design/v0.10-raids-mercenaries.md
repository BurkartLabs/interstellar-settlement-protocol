# Raids and mercenary contracts: lifecycle, windows, loot, escrow and reports (ISP-165, AB#859)

v0.10 Warfare (Feature AB#394) turns the combat model (`docs/design/v0.10-combat.md`, ISP-164, AB#858) into the first
system where one player takes from another. This file designs the two things that use it: the **raid** (declare,
travel, warn, resolve, return) and the **mercenary contract** (a fleet hired for a term, with escrow). It also fixes
the three numbers the combat design left to it: what a raid takes, the warning window, and the r that fleet upkeep is
measured against.

It changes no code. It was read against ISP main `c559bace`, 2026-09-27. The mercenary contract is already built
(ISP-176, AB#870, PR 1008) on assumptions of its own, because this file did not exist yet: section 7 reconciles with
what it built, point by point, and says plainly where this design disagrees.

**The Board's answer (D-0.10-1, AB#705, closed):** *"14 day new player protection. No offline protection. In a raid,
buildings can be damaged (costs money to repair), units destroyed, resources taken."* The acceptance of this story was
written before the answer and asks for loot, losses, protection and cooldown as the Decision's options, "pending
Board". They are no longer pending: section 3 applies the answer, and marks as **assumed** only what the answer leaves
open (each the more protective reading, each a number the Board can change or remove).

**Never for real money.** The Board ruled on AB#706: no v0.10 military mechanic may ever be sold or accelerated for real
money (BRIEF.md, rule 3). That covers every raid rule, window, cap and contract term here. No number has a paid path.

## 1. What a raid targets

Players do not own stations until v0.12, so a raid does not target a station. It targets **one corporation's holdings
at one station**:

- **Its garrison there:** the defense troops standing at that station (`corp.military.garrisons[stationId]`), its own
  fleets stationed there, and fleets it has hired whose contract permits defense and which stand there.
- **Its station inventory there:** `corp.inventory[stationId]`, valued at the NPC standing bid.
- **Its buildings there.** Buildings carry no station today (`docs/design/buildings.md`). For raids, a building stands
  at a station by this rule, which ISP-179 implements as `buildingsAtStation(corp, stationId)`: a building on a lease
  (an extractor, a lease facility) stands at the station whose body is the lease's body; every other building stands
  at the corporation's home station, the one it was founded at. **Assumed:** a corporation's home station never moves.

Never a target, whatever the answer allows elsewhere: **credits** (a raid takes resources, and credits are not a
resource), research, the level, leases themselves, the office, and the Headquarters. The corporation is never removed.

## 2. The raid lifecycle

```
            declare                         arrival (tick)                    return (tick)
 (none) ────────────▶ inbound ─────────────────────────▶ resolved ─────────────────────▶ completed
                         │  recall (attacker)                │ draw or loss: nothing taken
                         ▼                                    ▼
                      recalled ──── return (tick) ──▶ completed            (the fleet flies home either way)
                         ▲
                         └─ turned back at arrival: target in its post-raid cooldown (section 3)
```

| state | what it is | who acts |
|---|---|---|
| `inbound` | declared; the attacking fleet is on its way; the defender has been warned | attacker may recall; defender may reinforce (ISP-180) |
| `resolved` | the battle has been fought at arrival; losses applied, loot in item escrow, reports written | nobody: the fleet is returning |
| `recalled` | the attacker turned back before arrival, or the raid was turned back at arrival | nobody |
| `completed` | the fleet is home; loot delivered to the attacker's inventory at the origin station | nobody |

**Declare** (`POST .../raids`, ISP-178). The attacker names one of its fleets stationed at an origin station, and a
target corporation and station, by public ids. The server checks everything in section 4; on success the fleet leaves
at once (it can take no other order until it is home), and the raid is persisted.

**The warning window.** The defender is told at declaration: an in-game notification naming the attacking corporation,
the target station, the arrival time, and **the attacking fleet's composition** (its units by type). Arrival is:

    arrivesAt = declaredAt + max(travel time, 4 hours)

Travel time is the fleet's own (`server/travel.js`: a minute on the same body, 10 to 35 minutes across a system, an hour
a hop between systems), and those are too short to be a warning, so the window has a floor of **4 hours** (assumed).
Showing the composition is what makes the window worth something: the combat model's counters (section 2 of the combat
design) mean a defender who knows what is coming can answer it, with the right garrison, a reinforcement, or a hired
fleet. That is the counter-play the combat design's worked battle ends on (15 defense troops instead of 10 turn a win
into a draw).

**Nothing requires the defender to be online** (DEPTH: nothing may punish absence). The Board ruled "no offline
protection": an offline corporation is not shielded. Its garrison fights automatically at arrival with whatever stands
there, exactly as an online one's does; being online only adds the choice to reinforce. No rule reads whether a player
is online, has a session, or has read the warning.

**Resolve** (the tick at `arrivesAt`, ISP-179). Eligibility is checked again (section 4); a target now in its
post-raid cooldown turns the raid back (`recalled`, nothing lost, it still counts against the repeat limit). Otherwise
the combat resolver (ISP-168) fights the attacking fleet against everything in section 1's garrison, at each side's
modifiers and readiness. Then:

- **Losses:** each side's units lost are destroyed, per the resolver. Hired units' losses are recorded against their
  contract (`recordContractLosses`, AB#870) and paid for from its collateral at the contract's end (section 7).
- **Attacker wins** (units left, the defender has none): loot is taken (section 3) and buildings are damaged
  (section 3).
- **Draw or defender wins:** nothing is taken and nothing is damaged (the combat design: a draw is a failed raid).
- **Reports** are written to both sides (section 5), and the post-raid cooldown starts for the target.

**Return.** Win or lose, the fleet's survivors fly home on the same travel time, carrying the loot in item escrow.
At `returnsAt` the loot is delivered to the attacker's inventory at the origin station and the raid is `completed`.
**Recall** (`POST .../raids/:raidId/recall`) is the attacker's until arrival: the fleet turns round and is home after
the time it had flown.

**Persistence.** A raid is a record in world state (`market.raids[]` beside `market.mercenaryOffers`), and every step is
driven by the tick from stored times, so a restart at any point resumes where it was (ISP-178 tests a restart
mid-travel).

**No escalation.** v0.10 has no war declarations, no alliances at war, and no retaliation rights: a raid is one fleet,
one target, one battle. A defender may raid back like anyone else, under the same rules. Repeated raiding is bounded by
section 3's cooldown and repeat limit, not by a war state. (Conglomerates, v0.12, are where escalation would belong.)

## 3. What a raid takes, and who is protected

The Board's answer, and what each part becomes:

| the Board said | this design | assumed (Board may change) |
|---|---|---|
| "14 day new player protection" | a corporation founded less than 14 days ago cannot be targeted | it cannot declare a raid either, until its own 14 days are up (the PM's reading on ISP-178) |
| "No offline protection" | nothing reads whether a defender is online; the garrison fights at arrival | none |
| "units destroyed" | both sides lose what the resolver says they lose | none |
| "resources taken" | station inventory at the target station, on an attacker win, within the cap below | credits are not resources and are never taken |
| "buildings can be damaged (costs money to repair)" | the target station's buildings lose condition on an attacker win; repair costs credits | the damage per raid, the floor, and the repair price below |
| (not answered) | after a resolved raid, the target has **24 hours** of protection | the cooldown, and its length |
| (not answered) | one attacker may declare at most **2 raids per 7 days** on the same target | the repeat limit, and its numbers |

The cooldown and the repeat limit were in the Decision's recommended option and are kept as the anti-harassment rules
for an all-ages game; the Board's answer did not remove them, and they cost a legitimate raider almost nothing.
Being raided grants no new-player protection again.

**Loot** (assumed numbers). Let V be the value of the target's inventory at the target station at the NPC bid:

    takeable = 20% × max(0, V − 10,000)
    carried  = sum over the attacker's surviving units of supply × 1,500 credits      (its hold)
    loot     = min(takeable, carried)

- The first **10,000 credits** of goods at a station are never takeable: a small corporation's working stock survives.
- **20%** per raid bounds any one loss, and with the cooldown a target loses at most 20% a day.
- The hold makes a raid's size matter: one surviving light fighter carries 1,500 credits, not the whole cap.
- Loot is taken **from every stack in the same proportion**, rounding each down (so the defender is left a balanced
  store, and the attacker cannot pick the most valuable goods).
- The attacker's gain equals the defender's loss exactly: the goods move through an item escrow (section 6), never
  created or destroyed.

**A worked raid.** The combat design's battle: 6 destroyers and 12 attack troops against 14 light fighters and 10
defense troops; the attacker wins and keeps 5 destroyers and 2 attack troops (22 supply). The defender holds 60,000
credits of goods at the station. takeable = 20% × 50,000 = 10,000; carried = 22 × 1,500 = 33,000; loot = **10,000**.
Against 15 defense troops the battle is a draw and the loot is 0.

**Building damage** (assumed numbers). On an attacker win, every building standing at the target station (section 1)
except the Headquarters loses condition:

    damage  = min(20, 4 × surviving siege engines) condition points          (siege engines are the building-breakers)
    condition never falls below 50                                           (a damaged building still stands)

A building's output is multiplied by condition ÷ 100 until it is repaired (an extractor at 80 condition mines 80%).
**Repair** (a new route, the building-damage story) restores full condition at once for `(100 − condition)% × the
building's cost basis × 25%`, paid through `settleTransfer` to the `fee` sink. It is never destroyed and never
removed by a raid. The worked raid had no siege engines, so it damaged nothing; the same raid with 5 surviving siege
engines would take 20 points from each building at the station.

## 4. Who may raid whom, and when

Checked at declaration, and again at arrival:

- The attacker is the authenticated corporation; the fleet is its own (or a hired fleet whose contract permits raids,
  `mercenaryUseAllowed(contract, "raids")`), stationed at the origin, not in transit, not offered for hire.
- The target is another corporation, named by a public id that exists (an unknown or malformed id is 404, with nothing
  changed), at a station where it has holdings (section 1).
- Neither corporation is inside its 14 days of new-player protection (section 3; boundary-tested on server time).
- The target is not in its post-raid cooldown; the attacker is under its repeat limit against this target.
- A corporation cannot raid itself, or a fleet it has hired out.
- Declarations are rate-limited per attacker (**6 an hour**, assumed), on top of the repeat limit.

## 5. Battle reports: private to the two sides

A report is written to each party's own record, not to any feed. The old global combat report feed
(`createCombatReport`, `state.combatReports`) is removed by ISP-166 (AB#860, not built yet), which must land before
ISP-179 writes the first raid report. A third corporation asking for a report gets 404, whether or not it exists.

| | attacker sees | defender sees |
|---|---|---|
| who | the defender's corporation name, the station | the attacker's corporation name, the station |
| before | its own fleet; the defender's garrison by unit type (revealed by the battle, not before) | the attacking fleet by unit type (it was in the warning); its own garrison |
| the battle | each round's counts and losses for both sides (the resolver's table, rounded) | the same |
| after | its losses, survivors, loot by item and quantity, damage dealt per building | its losses, loot lost by item and quantity, each building's new condition and repair price |
| never | the defender's credits, research, modifiers or holdings elsewhere | the attacker's credits, research, modifiers or holdings elsewhere |

Every field is a number, an enum or an id resolved to a name on the server; there is no free text from either side,
in the report or in the warning notification (all-ages play, AB#707).

## 6. Security

- **Authentication.** Every raid and contract route is under `/api/accounts/:accountId`, so `requireAuth` and
  `requireAccountAccess` run first; the acting corporation is always the caller's own.
- **Input.** Every field is a number in a stated range, an enum, or an id checked against what exists (the
  `server/gameplayInput.js` table and the hostile-input tests); target ids are public corporation ids, never account
  ids, and nothing a report or offer shows names an account id or an e-mail.
- **Value moves only through audited primitives.** Credits move through `settleTransfer` (the v0.9 settlement ledger:
  escrow-hold and escrow-release for contracts, `fee` for repairs). Goods move through a matching **item escrow**
  primitive, `settleItems({ from, to, items, reason, idempotencyKey })`, which ISP-179 adds beside `settleTransfer`:
  the same idempotency, a ledger line per movement, and a refusal (with nothing moved) when the payer does not hold the
  goods. Loot moves defender → `escrow:raid-<id>` at resolution and escrow → attacker at return, each in one mutation.
- **Once only.** Every step is keyed by the raid id and the step, so a tick that runs twice, or a restart between
  steps, moves nothing twice.
- **Never for real money** (AB#706): no raid, window, protection, cap or contract term can be bought or sped up.

## 7. Mercenary contracts, and what ISP-176 already built

A corporation offers one of its fleets for hire on structured terms; another hires it for the term; at the end the
fleet is back under its owner's command, the price is the owner's, and the collateral pays for any units lost. ISP-176
(AB#870) built this before the design existed, and flagged five assumptions for the Board. This design adopts the
built shape and says, for each, whether it agrees.

**The contract, as built** (`server/mercenaries.js`): fields are numbers and enums only.

| field | bounds |
|---|---|
| `termHours` | 12, 24, 72 or 168 |
| `price` | 100 to 10,000,000 credits, paid to the owner at the end |
| `collateral` | 1 credit to the fleet's replacement value, held until the end |
| `allowedUse` | `defense`, `pve` or `raids` (raids also permits pve and defense; pve also permits defense) |

**Lifecycle, as built:** `open` (offered; the fleet takes no order from its owner) → `hired` (price and collateral in
`escrow:merc-<id>` through settleTransfer; the renter commands the fleet for the term) → `completed` (at the term's
end: lost units leave the owner's fleet; the owner is paid the price and, from the collateral, each lost unit's
replacement value capped at the collateral; the rest of the collateral returns to the renter). `withdrawn` by the
owner while open; lapsed after 7 days open.

**The loss formula:** compensation = min(collateral, Σ lost units × replacement value), where a unit's replacement
value is its catalog credits plus its goods at the NPC bid; refund to the renter = collateral − compensation. This is
EVE's courier-contract collateral applied to a fleet (prior art, section 9): the renter posts what the owner stands to
lose, and the owner chooses how much cover to ask for.

**ISP-176's five assumptions, reconciled:**

1. **Terms, price range, collateral range, allowed uses.** Agreed, as built.
2. **Replacement value = catalog credits + goods at NPC bid; losses paid from collateral, capped.** Agreed. The cap
   means an owner who asks for little collateral underinsures its own fleet; that is the owner's pricing choice.
3. **An open offer lapses after 7 days.** Agreed.
4. **The owner keeps paying the fleet's upkeep throughout.** Agreed, and this design adds its consequence, which the
   build did not state: **a hired fleet fights at its owner's readiness** (the units are the owner's, and readiness is
   what unpaid upkeep costs). A renter could otherwise hire a fleet at 40% readiness without knowing it, so **the
   offer should show the owner's current readiness** (a change for ISP-177's screen and `offerView`, not built).
5. **No platform fee on a hire.** **This design disagrees.** Every other player-to-player instrument in v0.9 has a
   sink: the exchange's 8% tax, the Development Bank's 2.5% origination fee, the 2% share-offering fee, the 1% listing
   fee. A hire with no fee is the one way to move credits between two corporations that costs nothing, which is also
   the cheapest way to move wealth to an alt (the owner "hires out" a single fighter at 10,000,000). **Recommended:** a
   fee of **2% of the price**, taken from the owner's payment at the end to the `fee` sink. It needs a follow-up story;
   until then, the alt route is bounded only by the price cap. Put to the Board as Decision **AB#4644**.

**Two gaps the raid stories must close** (not decisions, but the built contract does not do them yet):
- The renter cannot move a hired fleet: it stays at the station where it was offered. A defense contract only
  defends if it already stands at the renter's station. ISP-180 should let the renter deploy a hired fleet between its
  own holdings for the term, and ISP-178 should let a raids contract's fleet be sent from where it stands.
- A hired fleet that is inbound on a raid when its term ends: the contract's end waits until the fleet is home, and the
  term is extended by that wait at no extra price (the owner cannot take back a fleet mid-flight).

## 8. r: what a fleet earns

The combat design assumed r = 45 credits per supply per day, the number its upkeep optimum is measured against, and
left r to this file. A fleet earns from raids (loot, less its own losses at replacement value), from hire (the
price), and from Rat bounties (PvE, not designed here). This design does not set r; the market does, bounded by
section 3's caps and section 7's terms:

    r = (loot + hire price − losses at replacement value) ÷ (supply × days)

**Assumed, for the probe to check:** the worked raid (section 3) committed 36 supply, took 10,000 of goods, and lost a
destroyer and 10 attack troops: 13,754 at replacement value (a destroyer is 4,294, an attack troop 946). **Against a
garrison that size, raiding loses money.** It pays against a weakly held station, and a well-held one is a bad target:
that is the defender's garrison doing its job, and it is why knowing the defence (the report after one raid, the
composition a warned defender reinforces with) matters. A fleet also cannot raid every day (the 4-hour window, the
target's cooldown, the repeat limit, and replacing losses), so r lands well below one raid's yield. The shape that matters for the combat design holds for any r above base upkeep u = 20 (there is an
optimum fleet size). **Target (bot number, P-PROXY, ISP-183, AB#877):** over a 30-day probe run, r lands between **30
and 90**. Below 30, fleets beyond the garrison are not worth keeping; above 90, the loot cap is too generous. Either
is retuned in these caps, not in the combat model.

## 9. Prior art

- **EVE Online courier contracts with collateral** (EVE University, *Contracts*): the courier posts collateral equal to
  the cargo's value; if the cargo is not delivered the issuer keeps the collateral. Section 7's contract is the same
  shape for a fleet: the renter posts what the owner stands to lose.
- **OGame's raids:** an attacker takes up to half of a target's unprotected resources, limited by cargo capacity, and
  newer players are protected by a score ratio. Here the take is 20% above a protected floor, limited by the hold,
  and protection is the Board's 14 days.
- **Travian's cranny and beginners' protection:** a store of resources raiders cannot take, and a new-player period.
  Section 3's 10,000-credit floor is the cranny; the 14 days are the Board's.
