# The debt layer: loans, bonds and the credit rating (ISP-144, AB#838)

v0.9's plan (DEVELOPMENT_PHASES.md, "Financial Markets") lists bond issuance, loans from players and from NPC
underwriters, and a credit rating exposed to lenders and investors. Feature AB#393 says they only make sense together,
so this designs them together: the records, the servicing formula, the rating function, the numbers, and why each
number a player sets is a decision (T-018). Equity (the share registry and offerings) is its own design, AB#839.

It changes no code. The stories that build it already exist (last section) and cite this file. It was read against ISP
main `00de7119`, 2026-09-26.

**Provisional numbers.** Every rate, cap and target below assumes today's demand model, which ECONOMY.md's "Demand: the
Board's rule (AB#2324)" marks as pre-tuning: the one 7,000 t/day Silicates order for the whole world. Feature AB#2374,
the demand retune, sets how much a corporation's yards actually earn, and that is what a loan is borrowed against, so
it will move the rates, the principal caps and the tap/sink target. The worked examples assume the retune's stated goal
(yards 3 to 6 pay) with margins **assumed** here and labelled so. The shapes, the formulas and the rules do not depend
on AB#2374, and the stories can be built on them now.

**Never for real money.** The Board ruled on AB#703: nothing in v0.9 may be bought or sped up for real money. No rate,
grade, fee, principal cap or window in this file has a paid path (AB#857 writes this into the brief).

## 1. Records

Every field a player sets is a number or an enum. There is no free text anywhere in these records (the Board chose no
player free text for all-ages play). Credits are whole numbers. Times are epoch milliseconds, set by the server.

### Loan product (`data/loan-products.json`, NPC underwriter's catalog)

| field | type | bounds |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | enum string | `dsdb-7`, `dsdb-30`, `dsdb-90` |
| `termDays` | integer | 7, 30 or 90 |
| `baseRatePct30` | number | rate per 30 days before the grade spread: 3.0, 4.0, 5.0 |
| `maxPrincipalMultiple` | number | of trailing 30-day income (section 4): 0.2, 0.5, 1.0 |
| `originationPct` | number | 2.5, charged once at disbursal |

The grade spread is one table for every product (section 5).

### Credit rating (`corp.creditRating`)

`{ grade, score, inputs, computedAt }`: `grade` is `AAA` to `D` or `NR`, `score` 0 to 1,000 (absent for NR), and
`inputs` the six sub-scores of section 4, each 0 to 1, with the raw values they came from.

## 2. Servicing

**The payment.** A loan repays in equal daily payments over its term (an amortising loan), at the daily rate
`r = ratePct30 / 100 / 30`:

    dailyPayment = round(principal * r / (1 - (1 + r) ^ -termDays))

The last payment absorbs the rounding, so the payments sum to the schedule exactly.

**Collection.** Each day at `nextDueAt` the servicing tick (ISP-150) takes `dailyPayment` through `settleTransfer`
(reason `loan-repayment`). For an NPC loan, the interest part is booked to the sink category `loan-interest` and the
principal part returns to the Development Bank's tap account. For a player loan, the whole payment goes to the lender.

**Arrears.** If the borrower's credits cannot cover the payment, nothing partial is taken. The payment is added to
`arrears`, with a **late fee of 5% of the missed payment**, and the loan's `state` becomes `arrears`. From then on,
`arrears` accrues **penalty interest at the loan's rate plus 15 points per 30 days**, daily. Each later collection pays
arrears first. The rating recomputes (section 4 caps it), and the borrower gets a notification. Nothing else happens
to an NPC loan in arrears, ever (section 10).

**Early payoff.** The borrower may repay `balance + arrears` at any time, plus a fee of 1% of `balance`, and the loan
closes. Paying off a closed loan is rejected.

## 3. What the rating is for

Every lender and bondholder prices off one number the server computes from the corporation's own record. Nothing the
client sends can reach it (ISP-147). A corporation too new to have a record is NR (not rated), and NR fails closed:

- the NPC underwriter quotes an NR corporation only its highest-rate product (`dsdb-90`) at the C spread, and at most
  25,000 principal;
- no player may lend to an NR corporation, or buy its bonds, without an explicit confirmation in the request
  (`confirmUnrated: true`), and an offer only reaches an NR borrower if its `minGrade` is `NR-accepted`;
- an NR corporation may not issue bonds.

A grade of D is "no new credit": no NPC quote, no offer accepted, no bond issued, until the grade recovers.

## 4. The credit rating

**The window.** The last 30 closed daily statements (AB#713: `state.statements`, one file per corporation, loaded by
`getStatements(accountId)`), each with `pnl.totalIncome`, `pnl.totalExpenses`, `pnl.net`, `balanceSheet.credits`,
`balanceSheet.totalAssets`, `balanceSheet.liabilities` and `balanceSheet.netWorth`. With fewer than **7** statements the
grade is **NR**. The 30-second financial snapshots (`corp.finances.snapshots`, 60 kept) hold 30 minutes of history,
too short to rate on, so they are not an input.

**The inputs.** Five are ratios in the spirit of Altman's Z-score. The sixth, leverage, reads the debt this design
adds; it is the one input that does not exist on main yet (`corp.loans[].balance` and `arrears`, plus bonds issued and
not redeemed at face). Each ratio maps linearly to a sub-score between 0 and 1, clipped at both ends:

| input | from | sub-score | weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| history | number of statements `h` in the window | `(h - 7) / 23` | 100 |
| cash cover | latest `balanceSheet.credits` / average daily `pnl.totalExpenses` | `days / 30` | 150 |
| margin | sum of `pnl.net` / sum of `pnl.totalIncome` | `(margin + 0.10) / 0.30` | 200 |
| solvency | latest `balanceSheet.netWorth` / (latest `balanceSheet.liabilities` + debt) | `cover / 3` | 200 |
| leverage | debt / sum of `pnl.totalIncome` | `1 - leverage / 0.6` | 250 |
| turnover | sum of `pnl.totalIncome` / average `balanceSheet.totalAssets` | `turnover / 3` | 100 |

`score = round(sum of weight x sub-score)`, 0 to 1,000. A corporation with no income scores 0 on margin, leverage and
turnover. The weight table lives in `data/credit-rating.json`, which ISP-147 creates from this table, and a test reads
the file rather than a copy (P-TWO-SOURCES).

**The grades.**

| score | 900+ | 800-899 | 700-799 | 600-699 | 500-599 | 400-499 | 300-399 | 200-299 | 100-199 | below 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| grade | AAA | AA | A | BBB | BB | B | CCC | CC | C | D |

**Caps, applied after the score:**

- `corp.finances.payrollArrears` above 0 (Bug #1097: wages owed): at most **B**;
- any loan or bond of the corporation in arrears: at most **CCC**;
- arrears older than 7 days, or any defaulted loan or bond in the last 30 days: **D**.

**When it is computed.** Whenever a statement closes (`closeDueDays`, daily) and whenever a loan or bond of the
corporation changes state. The result is stored on `corp.creditRating`. ISP-147's acceptance names
`financialSnapshotTick`; that tick runs every 30 seconds, and reading 30 statements from disk each time buys nothing,
because the inputs only change on those events.

**What others see (ISP-148, AB#842).** Another corporation's rating is read through
`GET /api/corporations/:corpId/credit-rating` (the corporation's public id, with a login): its grade, its score in a band
50 wide (`800-849`), and each input as a band, `strong` (sub-score 0.67 or more), `fair` (sub-score 0.34 or more) or `weak`. Never
the score itself, a raw input, a credit balance or a statement. The bands are in `data/credit-rating.json` (`public`).
The corporation's own Finance tab shows its grade, its score and one line on what holds it back most.

**Quoting uses the grade after the loan.** A quote recomputes the score as if the loan were already taken: debt plus
the principal, and total assets plus the principal on the assumption that it is spent on assets. The rate is set from
that pro-forma grade. This is what makes borrowing more cost more per credit (section 8).

## 5. NPC loans: the Deep Space Development Bank

The Development Bank already exists as an NPC building (`data/buildings.json`, `deep-space-development-bank`, today
coming-soon). It lends from a tap account: principal is created when lent and destroyed when repaid (section 9).

| product | term | base rate / 30 days | max principal (x trailing 30-day income) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dsdb-7` | 7 days | 3.0% | 0.2 |
| `dsdb-30` | 30 days | 4.0% | 0.5 |
| `dsdb-90` | 90 days | 5.0% | 1.0 |

**The spread by pro-forma grade, added to the base, per 30 days:**

| AAA | AA | A | BBB | BB | B | CCC | CC | C | D | NR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +0 | +0.5 | +1 | +2 | +3 | +5 | +8 | +12 | +16 | no credit | `dsdb-90` only, at +16, at most 25,000 |

Origination is 2.5% of principal, taken as a fee (reason `fee`, to the sink) at disbursal. A quote holds for 10
minutes. Accept re-quotes on the server and refuses if the terms moved (ISP-149).

## 6. Player-to-player loans

A lender posts an offer (section 1), and its principal goes into escrow (`escrow-hold`). A borrower whose grade meets
`minGrade` accepts it, and the escrow is released to the borrower. Servicing, arrears and default follow sections 2
and 10, with the lender as payee.

- **Floor rate: 2.0% per 30 days.** No 0% loan can be written, so a loan is never a free gift between accounts.
- **Per-pair cap: 100,000 principal per rolling 30 days** between any two corporations, counting both directions and
  every loan between them. An offer or an accept that would pass it is rejected.
- **Self-dealing is rejected**: a corporation cannot take its own offer.
- A settlement fee of 0.5% of principal, paid by the borrower at disbursal, goes to the sink (`fee`).

## 7. Bonds

An issuer at **Level 10 or above with a grade of B or better** issues a series (ISP-154 rejects NR and D; this design
also leaves out CCC to C, whose implied yields make an issue a loan in disguise). During the window, players subscribe
at face. When the window closes, the **NPC underwriter** takes a share of the units still unsold:

    underwriterShare = clip((couponPct30 - (impliedYield(grade) - 2)) / 4, 0, 1)

So it takes none at 2 points under the grade's implied yield, half at the yield, and all at 2 points over. Units nobody
takes lapse. The issuer receives the proceeds (`bond-subscription`).

| grade | AAA | AA | A | BBB | BB | B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| implied yield, % per 30 days | 3.0 | 3.5 | 4.0 | 5.0 | 6.0 | 8.0 |

Coupons are paid every 7 days, pro rata by units: `couponPct30 x 7/30 x face x units`, rounded down per holder, with
the remainder to the largest holder so the total paid equals the coupon exactly (ISP-155). Face is redeemed at
maturity. A missed coupon or redemption is arrears (section 2), then default (section 10).

## 8. Every knob has an interior optimum (T-018)

A loan whose return is linear in principal is not a decision; it is "borrow the maximum". Each knob a player sets has
a cost that rises faster than its benefit.

**The example corporation (assumed, after AB#2374's retune).** Two yards, 900,000 income and 105,000 profit over the
last 30 days (3,500 a day), 150,000 credits, 310,000 net worth, 20,000 other liabilities, 30 statements. It scores 814,
grade AA. Yards 3 to 6 cost what ISP-309 prices them at (63,000, 78,000, 98,000, 122,000). Each is **assumed** to net
1,000, 700, 400 and 150 a day at tuned demand: each extra yard sells into a market the corporation's own yards already
serve.

**Principal (`dsdb-30`, over the loan's 30 days).** Borrow enough for N more yards. The pro-forma grade falls as debt
grows, and the rate applies to all of it. A daily payment the corporation's cash cannot meet goes to arrears (late fee
and penalty, section 2):

| borrow | yards | grade after | rate / 30d | payment / day vs cash / day | interest + fees + arrears | earned | net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | AA (814) | - | - | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 63,000 | 1 | A (770) | 5% | 2,155 vs 4,500 | 3,216 | 30,000 | **26,784** |
| 141,000 | 2 | BBB (650) | 6% | 4,847 vs 5,200 | 7,938 | 51,000 | **43,062** |
| 239,000 | 3 | BB (545) | 7% | 8,258 vs 5,600 | 27,475 | 63,000 | **35,525** |
| 361,000 | 4 | B (453) | 9% | 12,601 vs 5,750 | 60,995 | 67,500 | **6,505** |

Neither the minimum (0) nor the maximum the product allows (450,000) is best; two yards is.

**Term (141,000, compared over 90 days).** A short term is cheap in interest but its payment outruns cash flow; a
long term is easy to pay but pays the term premium for longer:

| product | rate / 30d | payment / day (cash 5,200) | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dsdb-7` | 5% | 20,277 | 19,347, and arrears run 20 days past the term: grade D, no new credit |
| `dsdb-30` | 6% | 4,847 | **7,938** |
| `dsdb-90` | 7% | 1,739 | 19,012 |

The 30-day loan is best here; the best term is the shortest one the corporation's cash flow can service.

**Coupon (grade A issuer, 100 units of 1,000, 60 days).** A low coupon leaves the issue unsold; a high one fills it but
pays more than the capital earns. The value of capital falls as more is raised: the first 60,000 buys a yard, the next
60,000 buys less, and beyond that little. It is counted at half over 60 days, for ramp-up and downtime (assumed):

| coupon / 30d | 1% (min) | 3% | 4% | 5% | 6% | 7% | 15% (max) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| underwriter fill | 0 | 25% | 50% | 75% | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| raised | 0 | 25,000 | 50,000 | 75,000 | 100,000 | 100,000 | 100,000 |
| coupons paid | 0 | 1,500 | 4,000 | 7,500 | 12,000 | 14,000 | 30,000 |
| capital earns | 0 | 15,000 | 30,000 | 40,950 | 49,200 | 49,200 | 49,200 |
| **net** | 0 | 13,500 | 26,000 | 33,450 | **37,200** | 35,200 | 19,200 |

**These are arithmetic from this design's own formulas and assumed margins, not play.** ISP-162's probe is what shows
whether a bot's choices land inside the range (P-PROXY).

## 9. Taps and sinks (provisional: AB#2374)

| flow | direction | where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| NPC principal disbursed | **tap**: credits created | from the Development Bank's tap account |
| NPC principal repaid | removes the tap | back to the tap account, out of the money supply |
| NPC interest | **sink** | `loan-interest` |
| origination, early-payoff, settlement and late fees | **sink** | `fee` (ISP-146's named sink account) |
| NPC penalty interest | **sink** | `loan-interest` |
| player loans, coupons, bond subscriptions and redemptions | transfers between players, neither tap nor sink | `settleTransfer` |
| NPC principal in arrears or never repaid | net **tap** until collected | bounded by the principal caps and the D grade |

A repaid NPC loan therefore destroys more than it created: its interest and fees. For a 30-day loan of 100,000, that
is 4.6% at AAA, 5.1% at A, 5.6% at BBB, 7.2% at B and 8.8% at CCC.

**Target (bot number, P-PROXY, checked by ISP-162, AB#856):** over a 30-day probe run, interest plus fees destroy **at
least 5% and at most 10%** of the NPC principal created. The floor keeps the bank a sink. The ceiling keeps borrowing
worth doing: above it, the principal knob has no interior. A world of only AAA borrowers would fall just under the floor
(4.6%). The target is provisional: AB#2374's retune sets what a yard earns, so it can move the rates that meet it.

## 10. Default

**NPC loans: non-destructive, always.** Arrears, penalty interest, the grade capped at CCC, then D after 7 days, and no
new credit. The bank never takes buildings, leases, inventory or credits beyond each day's scheduled collection, and
never removes a corporation. What it lent and has not collected is a tap, bounded by section 5's caps.

**Player loans and bonds: the Board answered Decision D-0.9-1 (AB#702, 2026-09-21).** The options put were: arrears and
the rating only (the lender's loss is their risk); credits and liquid holdings only; or credits, then liquid holdings,
then assets. The Board chose: *"Anything liquid, assets force sold if not able to cover."* ISP-286 (AB#993) builds it,
with the more protective reading wherever the answer leaves a gap:

- **Default trigger:** a player loan, or a bond coupon or redemption, more than 7 days in arrears.
- **Order:** credits; then station inventory, sold at the NPC standing bid; then bond and share holdings, bonds at 90%
  of face; then buildings and leases, force-sold to the ISA at **40% of what the corporation paid for them**. It stops at
  the amount owed: principal, interest, late fees and penalty. Any surplus stays with the debtor.
- **Grace:** a warning notification, then **24 hours** before anything is force-sold. Paying in that time cancels the
  sale, and assets cannot be moved out of reach in the meantime.
- **Never sold:** the office, research, the level and the Headquarters. A debtor is never removed from the game.
- **What "paid" means, and what a sale takes with it (AB#2569, server/buildingRemoval.js):** a building records what it
  cost when built and what each upgrade cost (`paid`). One built before that record uses the catalogue price plus each
  tier's price, which for an extractor yard is the first yard's price: it understates what was spent, never overstates it.
  A building goes with what is paired with it: an extractor yard's extractor and lease slot, a refinery's record, a
  facility's lease slot, and its asset value. Work under way: an extractor's cycle ends with what it mined so far
  delivered; a refinery's finished run is delivered and a running one is abandoned with its recorded inputs returned to
  their station; probe fabrications and ship builds already paid for complete as scheduled.
- **Bonds:** what is recovered is paid to holders pro rata by units.

## 11. Abuse

- **Moving wealth to an alt account at 0%:** closed by the 2% floor rate and the 100,000 per-pair cap per 30 days, both
  checked on the server. A wealth transfer through loans costs at least 2% a month and moves at most 100,000 a month per
  pair.
- **Self-dealing:** a corporation is never both lender and borrower, issuer and holder (rejected on the server).
- **Forged terms:** the server computes every rate, payment, fill and grade. A rate, payment or grade in a request is
  ignored (ISP-149 tests this).
- **Free text:** none. Every term field is a number or an enum (section 1).
- **Rating manipulation:** the inputs are the server's own statements. Moving credits in and out on one day changes
  only that day's cash cover, one input of six, and the solvency and leverage inputs see the debt.
- **Unsecured player lending (EBank):** a lender sees the borrower's grade and sets `minGrade`, and default recovery
  (section 10) gives the lender a claim on the debtor's assets, which EBank's depositors never had.

## 12. Prior art

- **Letter-grade credit scales.** Rating agencies grade issuers AAA down to D, with default at D; "not rated" means no
  opinion was formed. This design borrows the scale's shape, not any agency's method or name.
- **The Altman Z-score** (Edward Altman, 1968) predicts corporate default from balance-sheet ratios: working capital,
  retained earnings, earnings and sales, each over total assets, and equity over liabilities. Section 4's inputs are
  that idea on the numbers this game keeps.
- **EVE Online's EBank (2009).** A player-run bank whose chief executive embezzled a large share of its deposits,
  leaving depositors with no recourse. It is the known failure of unsecured player-to-player lending, and the reason
  sections 6 and 10 give lenders a rating to price by and a claim on assets.
