# Equity: the share registry, offerings and performance-linked returns (ISP-145, AB#839)

v0.9's plan (DEVELOPMENT_PHASES.md, "Financial Markets") lists shares in player corporations with "investment returns
based on target corp performance". This designs them: the records, the listing gate, how shares are offered on the
Intergalactic Stock Exchange, how holders are paid, how an investor exits, and why the one knob an issuer sets is a
decision (T-018). It is the equity half of Feature AB#393; the debt half, and the vocabulary this file uses (the credit
grade, the daily statements, `settleTransfer`, the `fee` sink), is `docs/design/v0.9-debt.md` (AB#838).

It changes no code. The stories that build it already exist (last section) and cite this file. It was read against ISP
main `7ad60c24`, 2026-09-27.

**Where it starts.** There is no equity today. `POST /api/investments`, which took an investor's credits, stored a
free-text `targetCorp` and paid nobody, was removed by Bug #689; `server/legacyInvestments.js` reports what it stored
at startup, for the PM, and leaves it in place. The Stock Exchange (`intergalactic-stock-exchange` in
`data/buildings.json`, faction IGSX) is `coming-soon`, `available: false`.

**The Board's rule (D-0.9-3, AB#704, answered 2026-09-21).** *"A company can issue shares. The value of these shares is
equal to the company value divided by the number of shares. As the company value increases, the share price increases.
This is a way for a company to raise money. But they can lose money."* Every price in this file is that formula; no
price is set by a player or carried in a request. What the answer leaves open (control and the stake cap) is section 10.

**Provisional numbers.** As in the debt design, every rate and cap below assumes today's demand model, which ECONOMY.md
marks as pre-tuning (AB#2324); Feature AB#2374's retune sets what a corporation earns and so moves them. The worked
examples use margins **assumed** here and labelled so. The shapes and formulas do not depend on AB#2374.

**Never for real money.** The Board ruled on AB#703: nothing in v0.9 may be bought or sped up for real money. No share,
fee, gate or payout in this file has a paid path (AB#857 writes this into the brief).

## 1. Records

Every field a player sets is a number or an enum; there is no free text (the Board chose no player free text for
all-ages play). Credits and shares are whole numbers. Times are epoch milliseconds, set by the server.

### Share registry (`corp.shareRegistry` on the issuer: the cap table)

The one authoritative record of who owns a corporation. Every corporation has one from creation (ISP-157 migrates
existing accounts).

| field | type | bounds |
|---|---|---|
| `authorized` | integer | **100,000**, fixed for every corporation, never changed |
| `founderShares` | integer | 10,000, issued to the founder at creation; never sold, never bought back |
| `unissued` | integer | 0 to 90,000; starts at 90,000 |
| `holders` | array of holding rows (below) | every outside holder, one row each |
| `payoutRatioPct` | integer | 0 to 80, step 5; default 25 (section 4) |
| `payoutRatioNextPct` | integer or null | a change waiting for the next period (section 4) |
| `state` | enum | `private` (never listed) or `listed` |
| `listedAt` | time or null | set when the listing fee is paid (section 2) |
| `lastDividendAt` | time or null | |

**Invariant, checked after every mutation:** `founderShares + sum(holders[].shares) + unissued = 100,000`.
**Shares outstanding** = `founderShares + sum(holders[].shares)` = 100,000 minus `unissued`.

### Holding (`corp.shareRegistry.holders[]` on the issuer)

An investor's holding is its row in the issuer's registry. The investor's portfolio is every row that names it; the
server keeps an index of issuer ids on the investor (`corp.holdingIndex`), rebuilt on load, so the portfolio never
scans every corporation.

| field | type | bounds |
|---|---|---|
| `holder` | enum + id | `{ kind: "corp", corpId }` (a player corporation) or `{ kind: "npc", id: "igsx-mm" }` (section 5) |
| `shares` | integer | 1 to 20% of shares outstanding (the assumed cap, section 10) |
| `costBasis` | integer | credits paid for the shares held, for the portfolio's gain and loss |
| `dividendsReceived` | integer | lifetime, for the portfolio |
| `acquiredAt` | time | first purchase |

A holder is never the issuer itself (self-dealing, section 9). The founder is not a holding row: the founder's 10,000
shares are the owning player's stake in their own corporation, and a corporation paying itself a dividend moves nothing.

### Offering (`market.shareOfferings[]`)

| field | type | bounds |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | string | server-made |
| `issuerCorpId` | string | a `listed` corporation that meets the gate (section 2) |
| `quantity` | integer | 100 to the offering limit (section 3) |
| `pricePerShare` | integer | **server-computed at open**: `floor(companyValue / sharesOutstanding)`, at least 1; never from a request |
| `soldToPlayers` | integer | 0 to `quantity` |
| `soldToMarketMaker` | integer | 0 to what players left (section 5) |
| `feePct` | number | 2.0, charged on proceeds (sink) |
| `state` | enum | `open`, `closed`, `cancelled` |
| `openedAt`, `closesAt` | time | an offering is open for **7 days** |

### Company value (read, not stored)

`companyValue = max(0, balanceSheet.netWorth)` of the issuer's **latest closed daily statement** (AB#713: `getStatements`,
one statement per day, closed by `closeDueDays`): credits plus facilities plus inventory, less liabilities, which since
the debt layer includes loans and bonds outstanding. It is the server's own book, so nothing a player sends moves it,
and it changes once a day, when a statement closes, so it cannot be pushed around within a day.

`price = floor(companyValue / sharesOutstanding)`, the Board's formula. A corporation worth nothing has a price of 0.

## 2. Who may list, and who may buy

**Listing ("deliberately inaccessible to small operators", LORE.md, IGSA).** A corporation lists once, and only when all
of these hold:

- **Level 15** or above (`data/milestones.json`: six extractor yards, three at Tier 3, 557,000 Silicate mined). Bonds
  open at Level 10 (v0.9-debt.md, section 7); equity is the step after, for a corporation that is plainly a serious entity.
- A credit grade of **BBB or better** (v0.9-debt.md, section 4). An NR corporation (fewer than 7 statements) cannot list.
- At least **30 closed statements**, and positive net income over the last 30 of them.
- The **listing fee**: 1% of company value, once, to the `fee` sink.

Listing changes `state` to `listed` and opens the Stock Exchange's issuer view. It sells nothing by itself.

**Buying.** Any corporation at **Level 8** or above (a Refinery built) may buy in an open offering, except the issuer.
Level 8 keeps the exchange out of the first hours of play without keeping it from mid-game players.

## 3. Offerings

A listed corporation opens an offering of new shares from `unissued`:

- **Price:** fixed at open by section 1's formula. Because each share sold adds `price` credits to company value and one
  share to shares outstanding, selling at that price leaves the price per share unchanged; nobody already holding is
  diluted in value (ISP-158 tests this).
- **Size:** at least 100 shares; at most 20% of shares outstanding at open; at most what keeps the founder at 51% of
  outstanding after a full sale (section 10, assumed); at most `unissued`.
- **Cadence:** one open offering per issuer; the next may open 30 days after the last one opened.
- **Sale:** players buy first, any time the offering is open, in any whole quantity up to what is left and up to their
  holder cap. At close the IGSX market maker buys its fill of what is left (section 5). Anything still unsold returns
  to `unissued`.
- **Settlement:** a purchase is one mutation that moves credits from buyer to issuer through `settleTransfer`
  (`share-purchase`) and shares from `unissued` to the buyer's row. The 2% fee is taken from proceeds to the `fee`
  sink at close.

**The founder floor sets a ceiling on everything outside holders can ever own:** with the founder's 10,000 shares fixed
at 51% or more, outstanding can never exceed 19,607 shares, so outside holders together hold at most 9,607.

## 4. Returns: dividends from performance

Weekly, on the day of the week the corporation listed, the issuer pays a dividend:

```
netIncome7   = sum(pnl.net) over the issuer's last 7 closed statements
pool         = floor(payoutRatioPct / 100 x max(0, netIncome7))      # a loss-making week pays nothing
toHolders    = floor(pool x outside shares / shares outstanding)     # the founder's part stays in the corporation
holder i gets its pro-rata part of toHolders, by largest remainder, so the parts sum exactly to toHolders
```

Dividends move through `settleTransfer` (`dividend`), issuer to holder, and add to `dividendsReceived`. An issuer with
fewer than 7 closed statements pays nothing. If the issuer's credits cannot cover `toHolders`, it pays what it has and
the rest is not owed: a dividend is a share of profit, not a debt, and the unpaid week shows on the Exchange.

**The payout ratio** is the issuer's one knob: 0 to 80%, step 5, default 25. A change applies from the next weekly
period, never to the one in progress, and may be made once per 7 days.

**Value.** A holding is worth `shares x price` (section 1). It rises as the issuer's net worth grows and falls when it
shrinks: profits retained raise it, losses lower it, and nothing else does. It is an asset on the investor's own balance
sheet (ISP-1), at that value.

### Worked example (assumed margins)

**The issuer, at listing.** Level 15, grade A, net worth 1,200,000, 4,000 a day of net income (28,000 a week, 120,000
over 30 days), founder's 10,000 shares only. Price: 1,200,000 / 10,000 = **120**.

**The offering.** 2,000 new shares (20% of 10,000) at 120. They all sell: 240,000 raised. Outstanding 12,000, company
value 1,440,000, price still 1,440,000 / 12,000 = **120**. An investor, B, bought 500 of them for 60,000.

**A profitable week (payout 25%).** `pool` = 25% of 28,000 = 7,000. Outside shares are 2,000 of 12,000, so `toHolders`
= 1,166; B's 500, a quarter of the outside shares, get **291** (a quarter is 291.5; the credit left over goes by largest
remainder). The founder's 5,834 stays in the corporation.

**After 30 days.** Four weekly dividends: B has received 1,164. The issuer earned 120,000 and paid out 4,664, so its net
worth is about 1,555,000 and the price is floor(1,555,000 / 12,000) = **129**. B's holding is worth 500 x 129 = 64,500:
with dividends, 65,664 for 60,000, **+9.4%** in 30 days.

**A losing week.** The issuer loses 2,000 a day for 7 days. `netIncome7` is -14,000, so the pool is **0**: nobody is
paid. Net worth falls by 14,000 and the price by about 1; had this week come first, B's holding would be worth 500 x 118
= 59,000, **below its cost**. Holders can lose money, as the Board's answer says.

## 5. The IGSX market maker: demand, and the exit

The Stock Exchange is an NPC building, and its NPC is the market maker (`igsx-mm`): the buyer of last resort that makes
an offering fill and an exit possible without a player order book.

**At an offering's close** it buys a fill of what players left:

```
expectedYield30 = payoutRatioPct / 100 x max(0, trailing 30-day net income) / companyValue
fill            = clip((expectedYield30 - 0.5%) / 2%, 0, 1) x gradeFactor
gradeFactor     = 1 at A or better, 0.75 at BBB
```

So an issuer that pays out nothing raises nothing from the market maker, and one whose expected yield reaches 2.5% a
month fills completely. The market maker is an outside holder like any other: it is paid dividends, and it is held to
the 20% cap.

**The exit.** An investor sells shares back to the market maker at `floor(price x 0.95)`: the Board's price less a 5%
spread. The market maker buys at most 1% of the issuer's shares outstanding per day across all sellers, and never above
its own 20% cap; when either limit is reached, the investor holds until the next day or until the cap frees up. Shares
it buys stay in its row; it never sells them to anyone, so they only ever pay dividends back.

**No secondary market in v0.9.** A player-to-player order book is not needed for an investor to get out: the market maker
gives a formula exit, and holding is always allowed. If ISP-162's probe shows investors stuck at the market maker's
limits, a secondary book is a new story for the PM to file (ISP-160's acceptance already says it is not built inside
another story).

## 6. The payout ratio has an interior optimum (T-018)

A dividend is cash leaving the issuer, so the ratio looks like "pay nothing". It is not: the market maker's demand for
the next offering follows the yield, and it saturates. Paying out more buys capital, until the offering fills; beyond
that, it only costs. Capital's value falls as more is raised: the first 120,000 is **assumed** to earn 40% over 60 days
(a yard and its ramp-up), the next 120,000 20%.

**The worked issuer choosing its ratio for its first offering (2,000 shares at 120), over the 60 days after it closes:**

| payout ratio | 0% | 5% | 10% | 15% | 20% | **25%** | 40% | 80% (max) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| expected yield / 30 days | 0.0% | 0.5% | 1.0% | 1.5% | 2.0% | 2.5% | 4.0% | 8.0% |
| market-maker fill | 0 | 0 | 25% | 50% | 75% | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| raised | 0 | 0 | 60,000 | 120,000 | 180,000 | 240,000 | 240,000 | 240,000 |
| outside share of outstanding | 0 | 0 | 4.8% | 9.1% | 13.0% | 16.7% | 16.7% | 16.7% |
| dividends paid out (60 days) | 0 | 0 | 1,143 | 3,273 | 6,261 | 10,000 | 16,000 | 32,000 |
| capital earns (60 days) | 0 | 0 | 24,000 | 48,000 | 60,000 | 72,000 | 72,000 | 72,000 |
| **net to the issuer** | 0 | 0 | 22,857 | 44,727 | 53,739 | **62,000** | 56,000 | 40,000 |

Dividends paid out are `payout x 240,000 (60 days of income) x outside share`. Neither bound is best: at 0% the issuer
raises nothing, and at 80% it pays out more than the extra capital earns. The best ratio is the lowest one that fills the
offering, and that moves with the issuer's income and value, so it is a choice each issuer makes again for each offering.

**For the investor** the knob is the other way round: a higher ratio pays more now, a lower one grows the price. At 25%,
B's 30-day return of 9.4% was 1.9% dividends and 7.5% price.

**These are arithmetic from this design's formulas and assumed margins, not play.** ISP-162's probe is what shows whether
a bot's chosen ratio lands inside the range (P-PROXY; ISP-159's acceptance asks for an interior choice in 80% of runs).

## 7. Taps and sinks (provisional: AB#2374)

| flow | direction | where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| a player buying in an offering; dividends paid to players | transfers between players, neither tap nor sink | `settleTransfer` |
| market maker buys at an offering's close | **tap**: credits created, to the issuer | from the market maker's tap account |
| market maker buys at an exit | **tap**: credits created, to the investor | from the market maker's tap account |
| dividends paid to the market maker | **sink**: back to the tap account, out of the money supply | `dividend` to `igsx-mm` |
| listing fee (1% of value), offering fee (2% of proceeds) | **sink** | `fee` (ISP-146's named sink) |

The market maker creates credits and holds shares for them; the credits come back as dividends, slowly, and the fees
destroy some outright. Its taps are bounded by structure: an issuer lists once, opens at most one offering in 30 days of
at most 20% of outstanding, the founder floor caps everything outside holders own at 9,607 shares per issuer, and the
market maker's own stake is capped at 20% and its exit buying at 1% of outstanding a day.

**Target (bot number, P-PROXY, checked by ISP-162, AB#856):** over a 30-day probe run, the market maker's net creation
(what it paid, less the dividends and fees that came back) is **at most 10%** of all credits created in the run. The
number is provisional: AB#2374's retune sets what corporations earn, which moves what they are worth and so what the
market maker pays.

## 8. Default

The Board answered D-0.9-1 (AB#702): *"Anything liquid, assets force sold if not able to cover."* Share holdings are
liquid (v0.9-debt.md, section 10, after credits and inventory). A defaulting investor's shares are sold to the market
maker at the exit price, `floor(price x 0.95)`, with the market maker's daily and 20% limits waived for a recovery sale,
because recovery must finish. An issuer's own default does not touch its registry: its holders keep their shares, its
price falls with its net worth, and its dividends stop while it loses money.

## 9. Abuse

- **Forged prices:** the server computes every price, fill, dividend and exit amount. A price or amount in a request is
  ignored (ISP-158 and ISP-160 test this).
- **Self-dealing:** a corporation never holds its own shares, and the founder's stake is fixed; a purchase or exit that
  would make the issuer a holder is rejected on the server.
- **Moving wealth to an alt account:** an alt that buys shares pays the formula price, so buying moves no value. Dividends
  to it are bounded by its stake (20%), the payout ratio (80%) and the issuer's real profit, which the main must first
  earn; getting out costs the 5% exit spread. The debt design's per-pair cap closes the loan route; this route pays full
  price and gives up profit to use.
- **Pumping the price before an offering:** company value is the last closed statement's net worth. Borrowing raises
  credits and liabilities together; buying buildings turns credits into facilities at cost; neither moves net worth.
  Only profit does.
- **Free text:** none. Every field is a number or an enum (section 1).

## 10. What a stake gives, and how much one holder may own: Decision D-0.9-3 (pending Board)

The Board's answer on AB#704 settled the economics (section 1's formula; holders can lose money). It did not choose
between the options for **control** and the **stake cap**, so they stay open. **Pending Board:**

- **A. Economic only, capped.** Shares pay dividends and change value; no votes, no control. One outside holder is capped
  at 20% of shares outstanding, and the founder keeps at least 51%. This file and ISP-158 assume A.
- **B. Influence without control.** As A, and a holder above 10% sees the issuer's full statements and is notified of
  large actions; still capped.
- **C. Full control.** A majority holder can take the corporation over (a hostile takeover).

A is the only one of the three that can be loosened later without taking anything from a player who already holds or
has sold shares; B could come with v0.12's conglomerates; C turns a stake into a way to take another player's game.

## 11. Prior art

- **Dividend payout ratio.** The share of net income a company pays as dividends; the rest is retained to fund growth.
  Mature, cash-generating companies tend to pay out more and growing ones less, and John Lintner's 1956 survey found
  managers set payouts against long-run earnings and smooth them rather than chase each year's profit. Section 6 is that
  trade-off with a demand curve attached; section 4's once-a-week change limit is a mild version of the smoothing.
- **EVE Online corporation shares.** A corporation can issue shares, which carry votes, and can pay dividends from its
  wallet, but whether and how much to pay is at its directors' discretion, and the market for shares runs on trust between
  players. Investment schemes built on that trust, the 2009 EBank embezzlement among them, left investors with nothing to
  claim. This design takes the discretion away: the price is the server's formula, the dividend is the server's formula
  once the ratio is set, and the exit is a formula price, so no issuer can collect investment and stop paying.
