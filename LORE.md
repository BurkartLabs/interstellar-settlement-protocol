# ISP Lore — World & Setting

> *The stars belong to no one. That is why we have lawyers.*

---

## The World of 2147

Earth did not collapse. It bureaucratized.

By the mid-22nd century, sovereign governments had largely ceded authority over off-world affairs to a web of intergovernmental bodies, corporate charters, and treaty-enforcement agencies. The planet itself is stable — densely populated, governed by a patchwork of regional administrations nominally unified under the **Earth Assembly** — but the frontier is something else entirely.

The colonization of the Sol system and the first extra-solar settlements spawned an entirely new class of problem: who owns what? Who enforces what? Who arbitrates when a corporation drops a mining rig inside a disputed extraction zone seventeen light-minutes from the nearest ISA inspector?

The answer, hammered out across decades of conference tables and proxy conflicts: the **Interstellar Settlement Protocol**.

---

## The ISP Treaty (2098)

The Interstellar Settlement Protocol is not a government. It is a legal framework — ratified by 34 sovereign entities and 11 registered corporate blocs — that governs the rights, responsibilities, and limits of activity in space beyond Earth's sovereign orbit.

Key provisions:
- No body in the Sol system or recognized colonial space may be privately *owned*. Extraction and occupation rights are *leased*.
- All leases are granted by the **Interstellar Settlement Authority (ISA)**, a semi-autonomous regulatory body empowered under the treaty.
- Military force is legal within limits. The ISA maintains jurisdiction over "disproportionate" engagements. What counts as disproportionate is subject to ongoing arbitration.
- Corporate charters operating in ISP-governed space must maintain a registered presence at a recognized station or administrative node. No registration, no lease eligibility.

The Protocol is updated approximately every eleven years at the **Revised Settlement Conference**. Proposed amendments are lobbied, leaked, and traded like any other commodity.

## Factions

### Interstellar Settlement Authority (ISA)

The regulatory backbone of the treaty. The ISA processes lease applications, investigates compliance violations, certifies corporate charters, and arbitrates resource disputes. They are not a police force — enforcement is contracted — but they hold the keys to every legal extraction operation in the system.

The ISA's bureaucracy is vast and deliberately slow. Priority processing exists. It costs.

**Mood:** Cold. Institutional. Not hostile, but not your friend either. Every interaction has a reference number.

---

### Earth Assembly

The governing body that ratified the ISP and continues to oversee it diplomatically. The Assembly sets the legislative framework within which ISA regulation operates. In practice, it is where the real power games are played — Assembly mandates can override ISA decisions, and corporations invest heavily in cultivating Assembly relationships.

Direct lobbying is technically prohibited. Registered intermediaries exist for precisely this reason.

**Mood:** Political. Slow. Susceptible to the right combination of standing, contributions, and strategic patience.

---

### GEX Commodities Authority

The trading and settlement arm of the **Galactic Exchange**, the primary commodities platform for the Sol system and beyond. The GEX is responsible for market infrastructure, routing, settlement, and — through its standing buy orders — price discovery in thin markets.

The GEX Commodities Authority maintains daily standing orders for select raw materials (currently Silicates) to provide baseline liquidity. These orders reset each day and are filled in order of arrival. They are not charity — the peg prices are low. But they are guaranteed.

**Mood:** Market-neutral. Efficient. Transactional.

---

### Station Administration (STA)

The local authority at a given station. At Earth Station Prime — the game's starting hub — the Station Administration manages dock assignments, building lease registrations, and local service contracts. They are the face of day-to-day bureaucracy that the ISA is too large to handle directly.

The STA is the reason your office requires three forms of identification and a registered corporate charter before you can legally hang a sign.

**Mood:** Officious, occasionally helpful. Think: interstellar property management.

---

### Intergalactic Stock Exchange Authority (IGSA)

The regulatory body overseeing equity markets for corporate shares. Distinct from the GEX, which handles commodities, the IGSA governs investment instruments, disclosure requirements, and listed corporate shares. Access to IGSA-listed equity is heavily gated — you don't list on the Intergalactic Stock Exchange until your corporation is a serious entity.

**Mood:** Prestigious. Deliberately inaccessible to small operators. Worth pursuing.

---

## Earth Station Prime

Your starting location. Earth Station Prime is the largest orbital administrative hub in the Sol system — part commercial district, part regulatory node, part corporate thoroughfare.

It orbits at a standard L2 relay point, permanently docked with the ISA's primary Claims & Leases processing center, a full complement of registered corporate office suites, and the Exchange Hub terminal node that connects station-side operators to the Galactic Exchange.

The station is not glamorous. It is functional. The corridors are climate-regulated, the uplinks are encrypted, and the espresso is tolerable. What the address conveys — to the ISA, to competitors, to anyone who checks your charter — is that you are serious about being here.

## The Sol System

The starting theater. Mining leases in the Sol system are the cheapest available, enforcement is the most consistent, and infrastructure is the most developed. It is also the most contested.

| Body | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mercury | Planet | Extreme heat; proximity to solar collection arrays; contested extraction zone |
| Venus | Planet | Hostile atmosphere; specialized equipment required; high-risk high-yield |
| Earth | Planet | No extraction rights. Regulatory center of gravity for the whole system |
| Mars | Planet | First accessible mining body; silicate-rich; popular for early-stage operations |
| Asteroid Belt | Field | Richest accessible resource density in Sol; requires mid-tier equipment |
| Jupiter | Planet | Gas giant; helium-3 harvesting requires advanced tech |
| Saturn | Planet | Rings are resource-dense; most operators lack the fleet to reach it profitably |
| Luna | Moon | Contested. Earth-proximate; political landmine |
| Phobos / Deimos | Moons | Small; useful for staging; low extraction yield |
| Titan | Moon | Exotic materials; long-range; worth it at scale |

## The Tone

ISP is not a heroic space opera. There are no chosen ones, no galactic threats, no ancient evils.

It is a story about entities — some human, some institutional — competing for resources, influence, and survival within a system of rules that is sophisticated enough to seem legitimate and porous enough to be exploited.

You are not saving anything. You are building something. Whether it is worth building is up to you.

The ISA will send you a notification when your lease renewal is due.
