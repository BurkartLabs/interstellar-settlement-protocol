HMH Stands One Sale From Level 3

MARS BUREAU · Filed by staff correspondent · Cycle 2147.14

Helix Meridian Holdings enters the cycle with its promotion paperwork half-signed. Of the two conditions the Interstellar Settlement Authority requires for advancement to Level 3, one is already stamped complete: the corporation's research division closed out Basic Extraction Analytics weeks ago, filed, indexed, forgotten by everyone except the auditors who will ask for it again in Level 9.

The second condition is a matter of arithmetic and nerve. Three hundred units of Silicate must clear the Galactic Exchange under the HMH ticker before the Authority will so much as glance at the file. The corporation currently holds 142 units in the Mars lease warehouse and two extractors running warm.

“Level 3 does not care how the Silicate arrives. It only cares that it is sold.” — ISA Corporate Advancement Handbook, 3rd rev.

Sister bureaus note that the reward is not trivial: Level 3 lifts the employee cap to 45 heads, opens two further building slots, raises the fleet ceiling to 22 hulls and permits construction up to tier 2 — the first tier at which a building earns its keep rather than merely existing.

This publication takes no position on whether that arithmetic favours patience or immediate liquidation. It merely reports the ledger.

Editorial
On the Long Arc of Settlement

ISP is designed as a multi-year strategy arc, and this desk would remind newer executives that the design is deliberate. Corporation levels are milestone-gated, not experience-gated — there is no quantity of idle cycles that substitutes for a completed sale, a finished study, or a signed lease.

Each unlock expands market access, infrastructure depth, and interstellar reach in that order, rarely all at once, and never on request. The Authority has heard every argument for an exception. It has granted none of them.

— The Editors, Settlement Gazette
